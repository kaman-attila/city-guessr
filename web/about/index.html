About PinTheCity

PinTheCity is a free geography game about one simple question: where in the world is this city? It started as a side project to make learning the world map feel like a daily puzzle instead of homework.

Why it exists

Most of us know the capitals and a few famous cities, and then the map goes blank. Names like Kryvyi Rih, Mekelle or Rafsanjan belong to cities with hundreds of thousands of people, yet very few of us could point to them. PinTheCity turns that gap into a game. Each guess teaches you a little about where things are, and the daily challenge gives you a reason to come back and learn a bit more every day.

The game is designed to be quick. A full game takes a few minutes, works on a phone as well as on a computer, and needs no download or account. If you want to keep your best scores and your streak, you can sign in, but it is never required.

Who makes it

PinTheCity is built and run by Attila Kámán, a computer science student from Hungary. It is an independent project: there is no company or investor behind it, and decisions about the game are made with the players in mind. Ideas, bug reports and questions are welcome at contact@pinthecity.me.

Where the cities come from

The game knows about more than 64,000 cities and towns, every place in the world with at least 5,000 inhabitants. The list, along with each place's coordinates and population, comes from GeoNames, an open geographical database maintained by volunteers. Places with the same name are grouped together, so when you are asked about "Valencia" the game knows about every Valencia at once.

To decide how hard a city is, the game looks at how many language editions of Wikipedia have an article about it, using data from Wikidata. That number is a good measure of how well known a place is around the world, much better than population alone: plenty of large cities are almost unknown outside their own country, while some small towns are world famous.

We also filter out places that would make bad questions, such as districts and neighbourhoods of bigger cities, and towns whose names are just everyday English words, so every round asks about a real, recognisable place. City names are spelled the way the map itself shows them, so what you read at the top of the screen matches what you see when you zoom in.

The map

The map is drawn from OpenStreetMap data, the free map of the world created by millions of contributors, and served by OpenFreeMap. Country borders used inside the game come from Natural Earth.

Fair play

The daily challenge is the same for everybody, so its results only mean something if everyone plays fairly. Each player gets one try per day, and scores are checked on the server rather than in your browser. The results chart only stores how many players landed in each score range, never who they were.

Your privacy

PinTheCity collects as little as possible. You can play without an account, there is no tracking or analytics, and if you create an account you can delete it and all of its data at any time from the account menu. The privacy page lists exactly what is stored and for how long.
