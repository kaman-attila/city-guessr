How to play PinTheCity

You get the name of a city. You drop a pin where you think it is. The closer your pin, the more points you score. That is the whole game, and it is harder than it sounds.

A round, step by step

  1. Read the city name at the top of the screen. There is no photo, no street view and no hint about the country. Only the name.
  2. Find the spot on the map. Drag to move around and pinch or use the + and − buttons to zoom. It is a normal map with borders and place names, so if you know roughly where to look you can zoom in and check. Searching the whole world label by label, though, would take a very long time.
  3. Tap to drop your pin. You can move it as many times as you like. Nothing counts until you press the round check button.
  4. See how you did. A line is drawn from your pin to the real city, with the distance in kilometres or miles, and you see the points you earned. Press the arrow to go to the next city.

Five rounds, from easy to impossible

Every game has five cities, and each one is harder than the last. The order is based on how well known a city is around the world: we count how many different language editions of Wikipedia have an article about it. A city that more than a hundred Wikipedias write about is one most people have heard of. A town with an article in only a handful of languages is a real test.

  • 1 · EasyWorld capitals and famous cities
  • 2 · MediumBig regional cities
  • 3 · HardCities you may have heard of once
  • 4 · ExpertMid-sized towns all over the world
  • 5 · ImpossibleSmall towns, often far from anywhere famous

In the later rounds we also make sure a few large countries do not take over the game, so you meet towns from every continent instead of the same handful of countries.

How scoring works

Each round is worth up to 1000 points, so a perfect game scores 5000. If your pin lands within 10 km of the city you get the full 1000. After that the points fall away smoothly the further you are:

Distance from the city Points
up to 10 km1000
100 kmabout 940
500 kmabout 720
1,000 kmabout 520
2,000 kmabout 265
5,000 kmabout 35

This means a guess in the right region is always worth something, and you never lose points for trying. Getting the continent right already beats a random guess by a lot.

When several places share a name

Many names exist more than once: there are Valencias in Spain and Venezuela, several Springfields in the United States and more than one Santiago. If the name you get belongs to more than one city, the one nearest to your pin counts. After you guess, the result panel tells you how many places have that name and the map shows all of them.

The two game modes

Daily challenge

Every day at midnight (UTC) there is a new set of five cities, the same for everyone in the world. You get one try. When you finish you see your total, how your score compares with everybody else who played that day, and a chart of all the results. You can share your result as a row of coloured squares without giving away the cities.

Missed a day? Every past daily challenge is in the daily archive, so you can play the ones you skipped and look back at the ones you finished.

Freeplay

Freeplay gives you five random cities, from easy to impossible, as often as you like. It is the best way to practise before the daily challenge, or to learn a region of the world you do not know well yet.

Accounts, best scores and streaks

You can play without signing up. If you create an account with your email or sign in with Google, the game remembers your best score, your daily best (your top score of the day, which starts again from zero every day) and your streak: how many days in a row you have finished the daily challenge on the day it came out. Skip a day and the streak starts again from zero.

Tips for better guesses

  • Read the name for clues. Spelling often gives the language away: "-ville" and "Saint-" point to France or Quebec, "-burg" and "-stadt" to Germany, "San" and "Santa" to Spain or Latin America, "-pur" and "-abad" to India or Pakistan.
  • Aim for the middle when you are unsure. If you know the country but not the city, a pin in the centre of the country keeps you close to anywhere in it.
  • Use big cities as landmarks. Remember that a town is "a bit north of Lyon" or "between Delhi and Jaipur", and place it relative to a city you know.
  • Zoom in before you commit. A few kilometres can be the difference between 950 and 1000 points on the easy rounds.
  • Learn from the reveal. After every round the map shows the right answer. Glance at what is around it; that city may come up again.
